1. Getting Started

What is StockAdmin?

StockAdmin is an inventory management tool for small business owners. It helps you track your products, record what you buy and sell, and understand your profit at a glance. You do not need any accounting knowledge to use it -- just follow the steps in this guide.

Who is it for? Anyone who buys goods and resells them: clothing shops, shoe stores, electronics resellers, market traders, and so on.

How to log in

1Open your web browser and go to the StockAdmin URL provided to you.
2You will see the login page. Type your email address in the Email field.
3Type your password in the Password field.
4Click the "Login" button. If your details are correct, you will be taken to the Dashboard.
Note If you see "Invalid login credentials", double-check your email address and password. Passwords are case-sensitive -- make sure Caps Lock is off.

What you see after logging in

After a successful login you land on the Dashboard. This is the home screen. It shows a quick summary of your products, variants, stock levels, and total profit. You will learn more about the Dashboard in Section 8.

How to open the navigation menu

All pages in StockAdmin are accessed through the side menu (drawer). To open it:

1Look at the top-left corner of the screen. You will see three horizontal lines (the hamburger icon).
2Tap or click it. A menu slides in from the left side of the screen.
3Tap any item in the menu to navigate to that page.
4To close the menu without navigating, tap the X button at the top of the menu, or tap anywhere outside the menu.
Tip You can also tap the "Logout" button at the bottom of the menu to sign out of your account.

2. Products and Variants

What is a Product?

A product is the general item you sell. For example: Boot black or T-Shirt. Think of it as the name on the label.

What is a Variant?

A variant is a specific version of a product. Products can have multiple variants that differ in size, colour, material, or any other attribute you choose. Each variant has its own separate stock count.

ExampleProduct: Boot black Variant 1 -- size: 40, zipper: no Variant 2 -- size: 41, zipper: yes Variant 3 -- size: 42, zipper: yes

How to add a Product

1Open the menu, then tap Products.
2Find the "Add Product" form at the top of the page. Type the product name in the Name field. Example: Boot black.
3Optionally type a short description to help you identify the product later.
4Click the "Add Product" button.
5Your new product appears in the product list below the form.

How to add a Variant

Once a product exists in the list, you can add variants to it:

1Find your product in the list.
2Below the product name you will see a small form with fields for "Key" and "Value". These are the attributes that make this variant unique. Example: Key = size, Value = 40.
3To add another attribute (for example "zipper"), click the "+ Add attribute" link. New key-value fields will appear.
4Fill in as many attribute pairs as needed to uniquely describe this variant.
5Click "Add Variant". The variant is saved and appears under the product.
6Repeat for each variant you want to add.
Example -- Boot black variantsVariant 1: Key: size Value: 40 | Key: zipper Value: no Variant 2: Key: size Value: 41 | Key: zipper Value: yes

How to edit or delete a Product

Each product in the list has an Edit button (pencil icon) and a Delete button (trash icon) next to it.

Clicking Edit opens a form where you can update the product name or description. Click Save to confirm.

Warning Deleting a product will also delete all its variants and all the purchase, sale, and correction records linked to those variants. This cannot be undone. Only delete a product if you are completely sure you no longer need it.

3. Recording Purchases (Adding Stock)

What is a Purchase?

A purchase is when you buy units of a product to add to your stock for resale. Recording a purchase does two things:

1. It increases the stock count for that variant.

2. It records your cost, so the app can calculate your profit later.

How to record a Purchase

1Open the menu, tap Purchases.
2In the "Record Purchase" form, click the Product dropdown and select the product you bought. Example: Boot black.
3Click the Variant dropdown and select the specific variant. Example: size 41, zipper yes.
4In the Quantity field, type how many units you bought. Example: 15.
5In the Cost per unit field, type what you paid for each unit. Example: 25.00. Do not include a currency symbol -- just the number.
6Set the Date to the date you made the purchase. It defaults to today.
7Click "Save Purchase".
ExampleProduct: Boot black Variant: size 41, zipper yes Quantity: 15 Cost per unit: 25.00 Date: 17 Sep 2026 --- Result: Stock goes from 0 to 15. Total purchase cost recorded: 375.00 (15 x 25.00)

Purchase History

Below the form you will see a Purchase History table. It shows all previous purchases for the selected product and variant -- including the date, quantity, cost per unit, and total cost. Use this to review your buying history.

Tip You can record multiple separate purchases for the same variant at different times. For example, if you restock every month, add a new purchase entry each time. The app adds them all together to calculate your total stock and total cost.

4. Recording Sales

What is a Sale?

A sale is when you sell a unit to a customer. Recording a sale does two things:

1. It reduces the stock count for that variant.

2. It records your revenue, so the app can calculate your profit.

How to record a Sale

1Open the menu, tap Sales.
2Select the Product from the dropdown. Example: Boot black.
3Select the Variant. Example: size 41, zipper yes.
4Enter the Quantity sold. Example: 1.
5Enter the Sell price per unit -- the amount the customer paid you. Example: 45.00.
6Set the Date of the sale.
7Click "Save Sale".
ExampleProduct: Boot black Variant: size 41, zipper yes Quantity sold: 1 Sell price: 45.00 Date: 17 Sep 2026 --- Result: Stock goes from 15 to 14. Revenue recorded: 45.00

What happens if stock goes to zero?

If the quantity you are trying to sell is greater than or equal to the current stock, the app will show a warning message. You can still choose to confirm and save the sale. The stock count will go to zero or below. See Section 5 (Corrections) or Section 3 (Purchases) to fix a negative stock count.

Sale History

Below the form you will see a Sale History table showing all past sales for the selected variant, including date, quantity, sell price per unit, and total revenue.

Tip If a customer buys 3 units at once, you can enter quantity 3 and the sell price per unit. You do not need to record 3 separate sales.

5. Stock Corrections

What is a Stock Correction?

A correction manually adjusts your stock count without recording a purchase or sale. Use it to fix your stock count when the real-world quantity differs from what the app shows. Common reasons:

- You found damaged goods (use a negative correction, e.g. -2)

- You found extra units during a stock count (use a positive correction, e.g. +3)

- Units were lost or stolen (use a negative correction)

- You miscounted a previous entry and need to fix the balance

Important Corrections affect the stock quantity only. They do NOT change your total purchase cost or total sales revenue. This means a negative correction will lower your effective profit (because you have fewer units to sell) but the correction itself does not appear as a cost in the Charts page.

How to make a Correction

1Open the menu, tap Corrections.
2Select the Product from the dropdown.
3Select the Variant.
4In the Adjustment field, enter a number. Use a negative number to reduce stock (e.g. -2) or a positive number to increase stock (e.g. +3). Do not use the + sign for positive -- just type the number, e.g. 3.
5In the Reason field, type a short note explaining why you are making the correction. Example: 2 units found damaged. This is optional but strongly recommended for your records.
6Set the Date.
7Click "Save Correction".
Example -- Damaged goodsScenario: You inspect your stock and find 2 damaged boots. Product: Boot black Variant: size 41, zipper yes Adjustment: -2 Reason: Damaged goods found during stock check Date: 18 Sep 2026 --- Result: Stock goes from 14 to 12. Purchase cost (375.00) stays unchanged. Net profit will be lower because you now have fewer sellable units.
Warning If your correction would make the stock count go below zero, the app will show a warning. You can still confirm it, but a negative stock count means you have "oversold" or over-corrected. Use a purchase or another correction to bring the count back to a realistic number.

6. Remaining Stock View

What is the Stock view?

The Stock page gives you a live overview of how many units you currently have for every product and variant. It is the fastest way to see what is running low.

How to use the Stock view

1Open the menu, tap Stock.
2You will see a table with columns: Product | Variant | Remaining Stock.
3Rows highlighted in red mean that variant has zero or negative stock. These need your attention.

How remaining stock is calculated

The app uses this simple formula:

Remaining Stock = Purchases - Sales + Corrections
Example -- Boot black, size 41, zipper yesPurchases total: 15 units (bought on 17 Sep) Sales total: 1 unit (sold on 17 Sep) Corrections total: -2 units (damaged on 18 Sep) --- Remaining Stock = 15 - 1 + (-2) = 12 units

Using the search filter

If you have many products, use the search box at the top of the Stock page. Start typing a product name and the table will filter automatically to show only matching rows. Clear the search box to see all products again.

Tip Check the Stock page regularly -- for example at the start of each week -- to identify which variants need restocking before you run out.

7. Monthly Charts

What do the Charts show?

The Charts page shows your business performance over a chosen time period. It is the best place to understand your overall financial picture: how much you spent buying stock, how much you earned from sales, and whether you are making a profit.

Summary Cards

At the top of the Charts page you will see five summary cards:

Total Revenue
All money received from sales in the selected period.
Total Cost
All money spent buying stock in the selected period.
Net Profit
Revenue minus Cost. Green = profit, red = loss.
Units Sold
Total number of units sold in the period.
Units Purchased
Total number of units bought in the period.

The 3 Charts

  • 1. Units Purchased vs Sold -- Blue bars show units you bought each month; orange bars show units you sold. Use this to see if you are selling what you buy.
  • 2. Purchase Cost vs Sales Revenue -- Blue bars show what you spent; orange bars show what you earned. When orange is higher than blue, you made money that month.
  • 3. Net Profit per Month -- Green bars mean a profitable month; red bars mean a loss month. This is the quickest view of your month-by-month performance.

How to use the filters

1At the top of the Charts page, find the Start Month and End Month fields. Change these to select the date range you want to see.
2To see data for a specific product only, select it from the Product dropdown. Leave it on "All products" to see combined data.
3Click "Apply" to update the charts and summary cards.
Example -- First month of tradingTime period: Apr 2026 to Sep 2026 Product: Boot black --- Revenue: 45.00 (1 pair sold at 45.00) Cost: 375.00 (15 pairs bought at 25.00 each) Net Profit: -330.00 This is expected -- you have bought 15 pairs but only sold 1 so far. As you sell more, the net profit will rise toward positive.
Tip A negative net profit at the start is completely normal. It just means your total purchase cost is higher than your total sales revenue so far. As you sell through your stock, the profit will climb and eventually become positive.

8. Dashboard

What is the Dashboard?

The Dashboard is the first page you see after logging in. It gives you an instant at-a-glance summary of your entire business -- no filters needed.

The Dashboard shows:

- Number of products -- how many distinct products you have set up

- Number of variants -- total variants across all products

- Low / out-of-stock items -- variants with zero or negative remaining stock (these need restocking)

- Units sold (all time) -- total units sold since you started using the app

- Total purchase cost (all time) -- total money spent buying stock

- Total sales revenue (all time) -- total money earned from sales

- Net profit (all time) -- Revenue minus Cost across all time

Tip Use the Dashboard as your daily health check. If the "Low / out-of-stock" number is greater than zero, go to the Stock page to find out which variants need a purchase recorded.

9. Tips and Common Questions

Q: I recorded a purchase but I do not see the stock increase.

A: Make sure you selected the correct variant before saving. Each variant has its own separate stock count. For example, "Boot black size 40" and "Boot black size 41" are tracked independently. Go to the Stock page and check the exact variant you bought.

Q: Can I delete a sale or purchase if I made a mistake?

A: No -- records are permanent to protect the accuracy of your history. If you entered the wrong quantity, use a Stock Correction to adjust the quantity back. If you entered the wrong price, you may need to record a correcting sale or purchase entry. Contact your administrator if you need help correcting a financial error.

Q: My net profit shows as a negative number. Is something wrong?

A: Not necessarily. A negative net profit simply means your total purchase cost is currently higher than your total sales revenue. This is common when you have recently restocked. As you sell more of your purchased inventory, the revenue will increase and the profit will improve. Check the Charts page to see the trend over time.

Q: What is the difference between "Cost per unit" and "Sell price per unit"?

A: Cost per unit is what you paid to buy the item from your supplier. Sell price per unit is what your customer paid you. The difference between the two is your gross profit per unit.

ExampleCost per unit: 25.00 (you paid this to the supplier) Sell price per unit: 45.00 (the customer paid you this) --- Profit per unit: 20.00 (45.00 - 25.00)

Q: My stock is showing as -1 or a negative number. What happened?

A: This happens when the total sales and/or corrections for a variant are greater than the total purchases. Common causes: a sale was recorded before the matching purchase was entered, or a correction reduced the stock too far. To fix it, either record a new purchase to add units, or add a positive stock correction to bring the count back to the correct level.

Q: How do I find a specific product quickly in the Stock view?

A: Use the search/filter box at the top of the Stock page. Start typing the product name and the table will filter live as you type. This is useful when you have many products and variants in the list.

Q: What should I do at the end of each month?

A: A good monthly routine is: (1) check the Dashboard for any out-of-stock items; (2) open the Stock page and verify the counts look correct; (3) open the Charts page and review that month's revenue, cost, and net profit; (4) record any stock corrections for damaged or missing items you discovered during the month.

Tip Keep your records up to date daily or weekly. The more regularly you enter purchases and sales, the more accurate your profit figures will be.
